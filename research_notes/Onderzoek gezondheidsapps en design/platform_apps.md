## 1. Which metrics and summary scores does each platform provide, and how are they shown on the home/today screen?

### Takeaway
By 2026 every platform has a daily readiness/energy-type score on the home screen next to a load or "strain" metric and sleep: Google Health (Readiness + weekly Cardio Load ring + sleep), Samsung (AI Energy Score + tips), Garmin (Body Battery, Training Readiness, HRV Status, Training Status), Polar (Nightly Recharge + Cardio load status), Coros (Base Fitness / Load Impact / Fatigue / Intensity Trend). Apple has been the exception. A readiness score and an insights summary only arrive with the Apple Intelligence Health app redesign, which was pulled from iOS 27.2 and is now in the iOS 27.3 beta (Nov 2026).

**Garmin Connect**

Not all models have every metric; the Forerunner 165 and Venu 3 lack Training Readiness. — [Garmin blog (HRV status / readiness)](https://www.garmin.com/en-MY/blog/fitness/new-features-on-garmin-smartwatches-help-to-maximize-your-training/); [Garmin blog (training status)](https://www.garmin.com/en-PH/blog/fitness/training-status-2/); [Cycling Weekly](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/fitness/garmins-health-tracking-metrics-explained-understand-what-your-smartwatch-is-telling-you); [shoulditrain.com (secondary)](https://www.shoulditrain.com/blog/garmin-training-readiness-explained)

**Polar Flow**
- **Nightly Recharge** combines **ANS charge** and **sleep charge**, each compared with your own 28-day norm. ANS charge uses HR, HRV and breathing rate in roughly the first 4 hours of sleep and runs from -10 to +10. The status scale is very poor / poor / compromised / OK / good / very good. The daily exercise tip combines Nightly Recharge, ANS charge, sleep charge and **Cardio load status**. — [Polar manual (Ignite 3)](https://support.polar.com/e_manuals/ignite-3/polar-ignite-3-user-manual-english/nightly-recharge.htm); [Polar manual (Vantage V2)](https://support.polar.com/e_manuals/vantage-v2/polar-vantage-v2-user-manual-english/nightly-recharge.htm)

**Coros (EvoLab)**
- **Base Fitness** is long-term load (about 6 weeks / 42 days, exponentially weighted). **Load Impact** is short-term load (7 days). **Intensity Trend** = Load Impact / Base Fitness, with zones Excessive ≥150%, Optimized 100-149%, Maintaining 80-99%, Resuming/Performance 50-79%, Decreasing 0-49%. **Fatigue** is a 0-100 score with 5 zones based on the difference between the two. Per-activity Training Load = minutes x intensity (time in HR or power zones), plus Aerobic/Anaerobic Training Effect from 0 to 5. — [COROS EvoLab](https://coros.com/evolab); [COROS support EvoLab](https://support.coros.com/hc/en-us/articles/10515632017044-EvoLab); [COROS recovery metrics](https://coros.com/stories/coros-metrics/c/your-coros-recovery-metrics-explained)

### Inferences
- The Polar and Coros model (an acute vs. chronic load ratio plus a nightly recovery score) has become the common template. Google's "weekly cardio ring with target load" and Apple's "7-day vs 28-day load" are consumer-friendly versions of the same acute:chronic idea.
- Rings and gauges dominate the home screen: Apple Activity rings, Google's weekly cardio ring, Garmin's Body Battery gauge. These are scores with an obvious goal state, rather than raw charts.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain exact visual descriptions (colour, chart types) of the new Apple Health Insights cards, because full articles were unreachable.
- Polar's "Cardio load status" categories (detraining / maintaining / productive / overreaching) and strain/tolerance definitions could not be confirmed from the fetched results.
- No 2026 information found on Garmin Connect home-screen changes after the 2024 redesign.

## 2. Manual / after-the-fact logging: flows, activity catalogues, effort/RPE, and how calories or load are computed without heart rate

### Takeaway
Every platform lets you add a past workout, but manual entries are second-class. Apple puts them in the Health app (not the Activity app). Polar's manual sessions do not count toward Training Load or Recovery. Garmin's manual entries behave erratically for load. Fitbit/Google estimates calories from population averages, not HR. Samsung computes calories from duration and distance. Only Polar formally turns RPE x duration into load ("Perceived load"), and Apple multiplies an editable 1-10 Effort rating by duration. Housework and yard work are generally absent as types, with "Other"/"Workout" as the fallback. Fitbit is the exception: its legacy searchable list reportedly contained "carpentry", "mowing lawn" and "cleaning".

### Cited Findings

**Apple**
- Manual workouts cannot be added in the Activity/Fitness app. Instead go to Health, then Browse/Search, Activity, Workouts, and Add. The fields are Activity type, Calories, Distance, Starts and Ends. — [Apple Support HT213729](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT213729); [Apple Support 101952](https://support.apple.com/en-in/101952); [iMore](https://imore.com/how-manually-add-workout-data-health-app-your-iphone)
- Sources conflict on whether manual entries close the rings. One says they "retroactively impact your activity rings", another says they "won't influence the Move and Exercise rings". — [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-manually-add-workout-to-activity-app-from-iphone/); [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/how-to/add-workout-to-apple-fitness)
- The Apple Watch workout list falls back to "Other": "Choose Other when you can't find a matching workout type". With Other, or when sensor data is unavailable, you get credit equivalent to a brisk walk. Users have asked for gardening/yard work since at least 2019, and the answer is to use Other or send Feedback. — [Apple Support: Workout types](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/105089); [Apple Discussions 2019](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/250581637); [Apple Discussions 2025](https://discussions-kr-prz.apple.com/thread/256027531); [GardenRant 2017, older](https://gardenrant.com/2017/11/why-isnt-gardening-included-in-exercise-tech.html)
- **Effort**: after a cardio workout the Watch estimates effort using heart rate, VO2 max, and age, height and weight. You can adjust it in the workout summary (Digital Crown, then Effort) or on iPhone (Fitness, Sessions, the workout, Effort, drag the slider, Update). — [Apple Watch User Guide (watchOS 26)](https://support.apple.com/en-by/guide/watch/apd95450de2a/26/watchos/26)
- Automatic effort is estimated for about 17 mostly cardio workout types. Strength training and similar types get no estimate, so you must rate them manually on a perceived-effort scale. Load = effort x duration. — [DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2024/07/apples-training-load-vitals-watchos11.html); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/smartwatches/im-a-marathoner-and-have-been-testing-apple-watchs-training-load-feature-heres-the-pros-and-cons)
- There is a setting that prompts you to confirm or adjust effort each time you save a workout. — [Apple Discussions](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255789290); [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2024/12/05/add-effort-ratings-to-your-workouts/)
- Effort-scale band labels (e.g. Easy / Moderate / Hard / All Out) could not be confirmed from the results (see Gaps).

**Google Health / Fitbit**
- Google Health 2026: on the Fitness tab, tap "Log activity", or for a finished workout tap Log under Recent Activities. The Gemini Coach can log or edit by conversation ("Log today's 3.5 mile run, from 9am to 10am"; "Modify my high-intensity workout. I did lunges instead of squats"), from a photo or file, and asks clarifying questions about duration or time. — [Google Health Help: Coach](https://support.google.com/googlehealth/answer/16961408?hl=en); [Help: Ask your coach](https://support.google.com/googlehealth/answer/17053789?hl=en); [Help: Track goals](https://support.google.com/googlehealth/answer/14236914?hl=en-GB)
- Custom/coach workouts ask for an **RPE** rating when you finish. Whether manual past-workout logs also ask for RPE is not documented. — [Google Health Help: custom workouts](https://support.google.com/googlehealth/answer/15402636?hl=en)
- Engadget found it easier to ask the Coach to log a food item or retroactively record a workout than to do so through the app UI. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2180910/fitbit-air-review-google-health-ai-coach-testing/)
- TechRadar reports the AI "regularly hallucinates workouts that never happened". The migration reportedly turned past runs into generic "workouts", losing maps, pace and cadence. — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/nonstop-lies-from-the-ai-as-google-launches-the-pixel-watch-5-its-redesigned-google-health-app-is-leaving-fitbit-users-incensed-at-its-crazy-hallucinations); [PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/05/25/google-health-app-fitbit-backlash-missing-features-ui-changes/)
- [older, Fitbit era] Calories for manual logs came from "population averages within your personal information range, but not using your heart rate". An intensity slider (low/med/high) adjusted the estimate, and manual entries could overwrite tracked calories (double-counting concerns). One example: a 90-minute circus class came to about 300 kcal from HR but 73 kcal when logged manually. — [Fitbit Community](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Product-Feedback/Manually-added-exercise-should-use-HR-data/idi-p/1899128/page/2); [Fitbit Community](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Android-App/Manually-logged-exercises-not-using-HR-in-calorie-burn-calculation/m-p/1334743/highlight/true)

**Samsung Health**
- To log manually: Exercise, pick an activity from the list, "Enter data", then set start time and duration (some types also ask for distance). Calories are estimated from duration and distance. - Users report gaps in the type list (e.g. aquagym) and ask for a way to record lawn mowing. — [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/444740/manual-activity-gets-assigned-huge-training-load); [Garmin forum: lawn mowing](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-web/237723/ideas-on-how-to-record-lawn-mowing/1128658)

**Polar**
- Flow web: Diary, Add, Training session, then enter date, time, sport and duration. Manually added sessions do not affect active time, the daily activity goal, Training Load or Recovery Status, and are not synced to the device. — [Polar support: add session manually](https://support.polar.com/uk-en/how_do_i_add_a_training_session_to_polar_flow_web_service_manually)
- RPE runs from 1 ("very, very easy") to 10 ("maximum effort"). Perceived load = RPE x duration; typical values for a 60-minute session are 180-360. Ideally you rate about 30 minutes after training. Newer watches (Grit X2, Ignite 3, Vantage M3/V3) let you rate on the watch; others need the Flow app. Polar promotes Perceived load especially for strength or other sessions where HR understates the load. — [Polar Training Load Pro](https://support.polar.com/en/training-load-pro); [Polar UK](https://support.polar.com/uk-en/training-load-pro)

**Coros**
- Training Load comes from duration x intensity (HR or power zones), so non-HR manual load is not described. — [COROS EvoLab](https://coros.com/evolab)

### Inferences
- The industry-standard way to estimate load without HR is session-RPE (Foster), i.e. RPE x minutes. Polar implements it explicitly; Apple implements it implicitly (Effort x duration). This is the most defensible method for manual labour, DIY or housework entries.
- Calories for manual entries across platforms are essentially MET/population-based (type x duration x body mass). Fitbit and Samsung say so indirectly ("population averages", "duration and distance"). None document their MET table publicly in the sources found.
- Manual labour, housework, yard work and carpentry are an under-served category. Apple and Garmin offer only "Other"; Fitbit's legacy catalogue reportedly had them. That is a clear differentiation opportunity.

### Gaps
- Apple's exact Effort labels (the bands on the 1-10 scale) and the full watchOS 26/27 workout-type list could not be retrieved.
- The current Google Health activity-type catalogue, and whether the legacy Fitbit list (carpentry, mowing lawn) survived the 2026 rebuild, is unknown.
- Garmin's official manual-activity type list and the effort-scale range (1-10 assumed but unconfirmed) were not found in official docs.
- Whether Polar lets you add RPE to a manually added session was not confirmed.

## 3. Fitness age / healthspan-style features

### Takeaway
Garmin's Fitness Age (activity intensity, RHR, body fat/BMI) and Apple's new Health Age (VO2max, RHR, sleep, HRV plus optional lab values, in a 7-category Longevity tab) are the flagship "biological age" features. Apple's launch reportedly drew backlash for being "brutally honest". Apple and Fitbit both translate VO2max into age/sex-normed levels. Samsung pushes instead towards novel biomarkers (Antioxidant Index, Vascular Load).

### Cited Findings
- **Garmin Fitness Age**: on newer watches it uses activity intensity (vigorous intensity minutes), resting heart rate, and body fat % (from an Index S2 scale) or else BMI. Garmin advises at least 5 vigorous minutes within 30-minute windows at 70-85% of max HR. Forum users report the minimum shown is actual age minus 10, and that wearing the watch less raises RHR and therefore Fitness Age. Users have asked why VO2max isn't used directly on new models. — [Garmin blog](https://www.garmin.com/en-MY/blog/fitness/what-is-your-garmin-fitness-age/); [Garmin technologies](https://garmin.ae/garmin-technologies/fitness-age); [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/387771/fitness-age); [Garmin forum on VO2max](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/fenix-7-series/317854/curious-why-fitness-age-calculation-doesn-t-take-vo2max-into-account-as-firstbeat-itself-suggests-to-do)
- **Apple Cardio Fitness**: a VO2max estimate (range 14-65 mL/kg/min) from Outdoor Walk, Run or Hike workouts on Series 3+. ### Inferences
- Age-framed scores are motivating but emotionally loaded. Apple's reported Health Age backlash suggests a design should show the drivers and what can change (Garmin's "how to lower it" framing) rather than an unexplained verdict.
- Garmin's Fitness Age shows that a healthspan-like score can be built from three easily obtained inputs: vigorous minutes, RHR and BMI/body fat. This is feasible without VO2max.

## 4. Design highlights and complaints (dashboards, rings, cards, charts, colour, typography, navigation)

### Takeaway
Users and reviewers punish redesigns that reduce information density, remove customisation or force AI. This happened with Garmin's 2024 card redesign, Google Health's May 2026 launch (51% of polled users said it "looks better, but is worse to use") and Samsung's June 2026 redesign (colours "have no correlation to the data"). Apple's strengths remain simple goal visuals (Activity rings) and baseline comparisons (Vitals). Its 2026 AI redesign is still in beta after a pulled release.

### Inferences
- There is a recurring pattern across Garmin (2024), Google (2026) and Samsung (2026). Card-based, AI-summary-heavy redesigns lower information density and remove user control, which triggers backlash, and fixes then restore customisation and lost data views. Design lessons: keep user-controlled ordering and hiding of cards, keep compact high-density views available, make AI text optional and short, and keep colour meaningful (semantic colour tied to the metric or status).
- Single-number gauges with a clear goal state (rings, Body Battery, Readiness 0-100) are the visual elements reviewers rarely criticise. Complaints focus on clutter, verbosity and lost control.
