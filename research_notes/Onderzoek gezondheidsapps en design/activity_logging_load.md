# Achteraf activiteiten loggen (sport + fysiek werk) en belasting/strain schatten

Scope: scientific and practical basis for adding "log a workout or physical work afterwards" to a personal WHOOP-like app that already computes daily strain from minute HR (Banister TRIMP on heart-rate reserve, mapped to a 0-21 log scale) plus muscular load from strength top-sets.

Research-method note: pacompendium.com and several other domains could not be fetched from this session (DNS blocked). All Compendium values below come from search-engine summaries of the official pacompendium.com pages/PDFs. Each MET row is marked with a verification status. To make the table authoritative, someone with normal web access should check it against the official 2024 PDF: https://pacompendium.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/1_2024-adult-compendium_1_2024.pdf

## 1. 2024 Adult Compendium: MET values for physical work and sports (with Dutch names)

### Takeaway
The 2024 Adult Compendium (Herrmann et al., J Sport Health Sci 2024) lists 1,114 activities under 22 major headings. Carpentry and DIY are under "Home Repair" (06xxx), heavier work-related carpentry and construction under "Occupation" (11xxx), yard work under "Lawn & Garden" (08xxx) and housework under "Home Activities" (05xxx). Values range from about 2.3 MET (light remodeling) to 8.0 MET (carrying heavy loads), which covers the light-to-vigorous band. About two thirds of the ~60-item table below was confirmed against snippets of official 2024 pages. The rest uses 2011-Compendium values that have not been verified for 2024 and are flagged as such.

### Inferences
- For DIY and physical work, offer three intensity levels per category (licht / matig / zwaar) instead of a long list. The Compendium already groups many tasks this way (06126/06127/06128, 11038/11040/11042), and this lets the user choose intensity without knowing codes.
- "Framing" (stud walls, beams) has no own code. Map it to 11042 carpentry heavy 7.0 MET, or to 06050 carpentry outside 6.0.
- Padel is not in the Compendium. Use tennis doubles (5–6 MET) as a proxy and correct it with RPE.
- MET values are population averages for the activity. Within-person variance for manual work is large (pace, load, pauses), which is a strong reason to combine MET with sRPE and with HR whenever HR is available.

- The 2024 Compendium may contain a code for HIIT or "high-intensity interval training", but none was found.
- No snippet showed the codes for swimming, climbing and some yoga entries.

## 2. Session-RPE (Foster 2001): method, validity, typical values, conversion and Dutch labels

### Takeaway
Session-RPE load = CR-10 rating × duration in minutes, in arbitrary units (AU). It is well validated against HR-based TRIMP (r ≈ 0.50–0.91 across studies) in many sports, ages and levels. It works as a stand-alone internal-load measure and is the natural fallback when no HR is available. Polar and Apple both use exactly this formula (effort × duration) for their manual/perceived load.

### Cited Findings
- sRPE = RPE (CR-10) × session duration (min), giving arbitrary units. It is a single number reported retrospectively that reflects the mean intensity of the whole session. — [Haddad et al. 2017, Front Neurosci (PMC5673663)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5673663/)
- Foster et al. modified the verbal anchors of Borg's CR-10 to reflect American idiomatic English. — [Haddad et al. 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2017.00612/text)
- Haddad et al. (2017) searched the literature for 2001–2016. 950 studies cited Foster's method, and 36 examined its validity and reliability with the modified CR-10. They concluded the method is valid, reliable and internally consistent across sports, ages, sexes and levels, and can be used "standing alone", though some authors recommend combining it with HR. — [Haddad et al. 2017 (PMC5673663)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5673663/). Caveat: this is a narrative mini-review without meta-analysis.
- Impellizzeri et al. 2004 (soccer): individual correlations between sRPE and HR-based load (Edwards, Banister and Lucia TRIMP) ranged from r = 0.50 to 0.85 (all p < 0.01). sRPE was judged "a good indicator of global internal load". — [Frontiers Physiol 2020 citing Impellizzeri 2004](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2020.00480/pdf); [PubMed](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/23222076)
- Other correlations: basketball sRPE vs Edwards TRIMP r = 0.86 (95% CI 0.82–0.89); Canadian football r = 0.69–0.91; ballet sRPE vs Banister TRIMP r = 0.79. — [Sportske nauke i zdravlje (basketball)](https://doisrpska.nub.rs/index.php/sportskenaukeizdravlje/article/view/13470); [Frontiers Physiol 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2020.00480/pdf)
- Polar "Perceived Load" = RPE (1–10) × duration, rated in the Flow app after the session (some watches also allow rating on the device). — [Polar Training Load Pro support](https://support.polar.com/en/training-load-pro); [Polar HK](https://support.polar.com/hk-en/training-load-pro)
- No source was found that gives an official conversion factor between sRPE (AU) and Banister/Edwards TRIMP units. — (search result; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- **Typical sRPE values (illustrative arithmetic):** 45 min easy run at RPE 3 = 135 AU; 60 min strength session at RPE 6 = 360 AU; 4 h carpentry at RPE 4 = 960 AU. The last example shows that long manual work produces very large sRPE totals. Using duration linearly overweights long, low-intensity work compared with HR-TRIMP, which weights intensity exponentially. A strain model should therefore compress duration (see section 4).
- **Converting sRPE into a TRIMP-like value** (no published conversion found; this is a proposal):
  1. Map RPE to an assumed fraction of heart-rate reserve, for example RPE 1→0.30, 2→0.40, 3→0.50, 4→0.60, 5→0.70, 6→0.75, 7→0.80, 8→0.87, 9→0.93, 10→0.98. This follows the familiar ACSM pairing of "moderate" with roughly 40–59% HRR and "vigorous" with 60–89% HRR. The mapping is not validated per point.
  2. Banister TRIMP = minutes × HRr × 0.64·e^(1.92·HRr) (men; women use 0.86·e^(1.67·HRr)).
  3. Feed the result into the existing 0–21 strain mapping. Then the logged session lives in the same units as measured sessions.
  4. Optionally calibrate per user: for sessions with both HR and RPE, fit the personal RPE→HRr relationship by regression, and use that personal curve for future HR-less logs.
- Ask for RPE about 30 minutes after the session, as in Foster's protocol (a background-knowledge detail, not re-verified here). For after-the-fact logging hours later, accept the rating but mark it as less reliable.

### Gaps
- The original Foster et al. 2001 paper (J Strength Cond Res 15:109–115) was not retrieved directly. Its details come via Haddad 2017.
- No validated Dutch translation of the Foster CR-10 was found in this session.
- No published, validated sRPE→Banister-TRIMP conversion exists in the sources found.

## 3. How existing apps handle manual or after-the-fact activities

### Takeaway
No major app computes HR-equivalent strain for a manual entry with no HR data. WHOOP computes strain from continuous HR regardless of activity labels, so manual logs only label time and add nothing beyond what the strap measured. Its Strength Trainer adds muscular load from logged sets. Apple and Polar use a perceived effort (1–10) × duration model, and Apple auto-estimates effort for cardio workouts from HR. Strava's Relative Effort needs HR. Garmin stores perceived effort as a self-evaluation. Fitbit overrides tracker data in the logged window with MET-style estimates, which users report as inaccurate.

### Cited Findings
- **WHOOP:** strain is computed from continuous all-day HR whether or not an activity is logged, and deleting a logged activity does not remove its strain. Without the strap there is no strain, and a manual entry cannot take even an average HR. Workouts imported from other services did not receive strain even when they had HR data (DC Rainmaker test). — [WHOOP community](https://community.whoop.com/t/didnt-wear-whoop-during-activity-how-to-record-strain/329); [DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2022/02/whoops-health-integration.html)
- **WHOOP Strength Trainer:** "muscular Strain is calculated based on information provided by logging" (sets, reps, weight). One reviewer saw a strain of 10.6 with the feature versus 9.9 HR-only. Users can add Strength Trainer data to an activity after the session. — [WHOOP The Locker](https://www.whoop.com/TH/en/thelocker/how-whoop-detects-and-labels-your-workouts-activities/); [road.cc review](https://road.cc/content/review/whoop-superknit-40-290297); [gadgetsandwearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/?p=17592028). Caveat: partly reviews and forum posts.
- **Apple (watchOS 11+):** after a cardio workout the watch gives an estimated effort rating the user can adjust. The inputs are heart rate, VO2max, age, height and weight; secondary sources add GPS and elevation. The scale is 1–10. Strength training, yoga and similar workouts get no automatic estimate and must be rated manually. Ratings can be edited later in the Fitness app (Sessions → workout → Effort). Training Load = effort × duration, compared with a 28-day baseline. — [Apple Watch User Guide](https://support.apple.com/en-md/guide/watch/apd95450de2a/watchos); [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2024/12/05/add-effort-ratings-to-your-workouts/); [DC Rainmaker on watchOS 11](https://dcrainmaker.com/2024/06/apples-new-watchos-11-trainining-load-turn-by-turn-trail-routes-and-more.html); [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/apple-watch-training-load/)
- **Strava:** Relative Effort is based on time in HR zones, so it needs HR and Premium. No official source was found on whether manual entries without HR get a score. Strava does have a Perceived Exertion field (background knowledge; official help page not retrieved). — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/article/strava-relative-effort-52163/); [GearJunkie](https://gearjunkie.com/health-fitness/strava-relative-effort); [intervals.icu forum](https://forum.intervals.icu/t/rpe-fed-from-strava/3112)
- **Garmin:** after saving an activity, the user can rate perceived effort and how they felt, and the ratings show in Garmin Connect. Training Load itself is EPOC-based (third-party summary). Manual entries in Garmin Connect can include an average HR field. — [Garmin manual: Evaluating an Activity](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-17DE938E-466A-4746-BDBF-7A6FC1B3A32C/EN-US/GUID-A6D466A0-FC75-4B4B-9D98-6B437BDC6D01.html); [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/garmin-features/training/training-load/); [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-web/390560/activity-manually-entered-not-showing-heart-rate-or-steps)
- **Polar:** Training Load Pro separates Cardio load (HR-TRIMP), Muscle load (power) and Perceived load (RPE × duration, rated afterwards in Flow). It is the best-documented example of keeping several load sources side by side. — [Polar support](https://support.polar.com/en/training-load-pro)
- **Fitbit:** a manually logged activity overrides the tracker's steps, calories and active minutes for that time window with the entry's values. Users can enter calories themselves. Users report that the estimates ignore HR, for example 73 kcal for a 90-minute class. These are community reports and may be out of date. — [Fitbit community](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/One/manually-logging-exercises/td-p/1846501); [Fitbit community HR ignored](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Android-App/Manually-logged-exercises-not-using-HR-in-calorie-burn-calculation/m-p/1334743/highlight/true); [Fitbit product feedback](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Product-Feedback/Manually-added-exercise-should-use-HR-data/idi-p/1899128/page/3)

### Inferences
- Design lessons:
  1. Keep measured HR as the source of truth (WHOOP).
  2. Always offer an editable effort rating, pre-filled from HR when available (Apple).
  3. Show the perceived/subjective load as its own channel next to cardio and muscular load (Polar).
  4. Do not let a manual entry silently overwrite measured data in that window, which is Fitbit's weak point. If it does replace anything, show it.
- The developer's app already has the WHOOP + Strength Trainer architecture (HR-TRIMP plus muscular load from top-sets). Adding a Polar-like "perceived load" channel is the missing piece for HR-less logs.

### Gaps
- The official support.whoop.com, Strava help and Fitbit help pages could not be retrieved. Some WHOOP/Strava behaviour is based on community and review sources and may have changed in 2025–2026.
- Apple has not published its exact effort-estimation formula.

## 4. Combining sources: HR window vs METs vs sRPE, blending, double counting, kcal

### Takeaway
Use a hierarchy. If minute HR covers the logged window, the HR-TRIMP already counts that load, and the log only adds a label, an RPE and (for strength) muscular load. Without HR, estimate a TRIMP-equivalent from sRPE, or from MET × duration when no RPE is given, and add it to daily strain. For partial coverage, apply the estimate only to the uncovered minutes. Energy expenditure = MET × body mass (kg) × hours.

### Cited Findings
- WHOOP computes strain from continuous HR no matter what is logged, so logging is a label and not an extra load source. This is effectively a guard against double counting. — [WHOOP community](https://community.whoop.com/t/didnt-wear-whoop-during-activity-how-to-record-strain/329)
- Fitbit replaces tracker data in the logged window with the entry's values, which can lower totals when HR data was better. — [Fitbit community](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Other-Charge-Trackers/Manually-logging-exercise-reduced-my-step-count/m-p/3588737/highlight/true)
- Polar keeps perceived load and cardio load as separate, parallel metrics instead of summing them. — [Polar support](https://support.polar.com/en/training-load-pro)
- sRPE and HR-TRIMP correlate strongly (r ≈ 0.5–0.9), so they measure largely the same internal-load construct. — [Haddad 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5673663/); [Frontiers Physiol 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2020.00480/pdf)
- MET classes: moderate 3.0–5.9, vigorous ≥6. — [PMC11336313](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11336313/)

### Inferences (proposed algorithm, not taken from a source)
1. **Coverage check.** For the logged window [start, end], compute HR coverage = minutes with valid HR / total minutes.
2. **≥80% coverage:** load = measured HR-TRIMP (already in daily strain). Do not add extra cardio load. Store the user's RPE as metadata and use it for calibration (personal RPE→HRr fit). For strength sessions, add muscular load from logged sets as the app already does.
3. **Partial coverage:** use HR-TRIMP for the covered minutes plus an estimated TRIMP for the uncovered minutes only.
4. **No HR:** estimated TRIMP = Σ over uncovered minutes of Banister(HRr_est). Get HRr_est from:
   - (a) the user's RPE, via the RPE→HRr table or a personal calibration (preferred); else
   - (b) the activity MET: %VO2R = (MET − 1)/(VO2max_MET − 1), with %HRR ≈ %VO2R (ACSM equivalence; background knowledge, not verified here). VO2max_MET = VO2max/3.5. If VO2max is unknown, use an age/sex estimate. If both RPE and MET are given, a weighted mean such as 0.6·RPE-based + 0.4·MET-based is reasonable, since RPE captures individual effort and pauses.
5. **Effective-time correction for manual work.** Long DIY or work sessions include pauses. Ask for "active time" or apply a default activity fraction (for example 60–75% for carpentry/renovation). Otherwise a 6 h DIY day at 4 MET counts as continuous exercise. This parameter is an assumption that needs validation.
6. **Baseline subtraction.** A HR-less period still had baseline load that the app may already estimate (for example resting HR imputed during off-wrist time). To avoid double counting, add only (estimated TRIMP − imputed baseline TRIMP) for that window.
7. **Muscular load for physical work.** For heavy lifting or carrying (moving furniture, carrying bricks, 11050 at 8 MET), the cardio estimate misses local muscular strain. Optionally add a fixed "muscular" component scaled by RPE × minutes for "heavy lifting" categories, kept separate from the top-set muscular load.
8. **Energy:** kcal ≈ MET × kg × hours. Example: 80 kg, 3 h carpentry outside (6.0 MET) = 1,440 kcal gross. Net (activity-only) kcal ≈ (MET − 1) × kg × h = 1,200 kcal, so subtract 1 MET when the app adds activity kcal on top of BMR to avoid double counting resting energy. The Compendium defines 1 MET = 3.5 ml O2/kg/min ≈ 1 kcal/kg/h (standard definition, not re-verified here). The 2024 paper recommends correcting for individual resting metabolic rate (background knowledge, not re-verified).
9. **UI:** after logging, show "geschat" (estimated) next to strain from non-HR sources, as Apple marks auto-estimated effort as editable.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source was found that validates blending MET- and RPE-derived TRIMP, or the effective-time fractions for DIY work.
- No source was retrieved on the accuracy of wrist HR during manual work (hammering, gripping tools). Wrist-PPG artefacts during gripping are a known issue but were not researched here, and they could argue for preferring RPE even when HR exists.

## 5. Occupational vs leisure-time physical activity: the physical activity health paradox

### Takeaway
Leisure-time physical activity (LTPA) is associated with lower cardiovascular risk and mortality, while high occupational physical activity (OPA) is associated with equal or higher risk (Holtermann et al.). The proposed mechanisms are long-duration, low-intensity, insufficiently recovered work that raises 24-hour heart rate without building fitness. For the app, heavy physical work days should count as recovery-demanding load, not as "fitness credit".

### Cited Findings
- Holtermann et al. 2012 (BJSM): in Danish employees, OPA increased the risk of long-term sickness absence while LTPA reduced it. — [BJSM 46(4):291](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/46/4/291)
- Holtermann, Krause, van der Beek & Straker 2018 (BJSM, "The physical activity paradox: six reasons why occupational physical activity does not confer the cardiovascular health benefits that leisure time physical activity does"). Mechanisms include:
  1. OPA is often too low in intensity or too long in duration to improve cardiorespiratory fitness.
  2. OPA is often done without sufficient recovery time.
  3. High OPA over long periods raises 24-h heart rate, while high LTPA over short periods does not.
  Other hypotheses in the paper concern static postures and heavy lifting raising blood pressure, low worker control, and inflammation. — [BJSM 52(3):149](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/3/149)
- Holtermann et al. 2021 (Eur Heart J 42(15):1512): in the Copenhagen General Population Study (n = 104,046, ages 20–100, ~10-year follow-up), LTPA was associated with lower MACE and all-cause mortality, while OPA was associated with higher risk. Commentators note residual confounding (socio-economic status), selection and information bias, and possible reverse causality. — [Eur Heart J via nl.go.kr mirror](https://dbpia.nl.go.kr/eurheartj/article/42/15/1512/6213773); [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/paper-probes-physical-activity-paradox-and-perils-privileged-advice); [NFA/Elsevier Pure](https://nfa.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-health-paradox-of-occupational-and-leisure-time-physical-acti/)

### Inferences
- **For recovery modelling:** a long DIY or physical-work day should add to strain and recovery need (elevated HR over many hours, muscular fatigue, sleep need). It should not be treated as equivalent to a cardio workout for fitness or VO2max progress. Tag these logs as "werk/klus" (domain = occupational or household) separately from "sport/training" (domain = leisure).
- **For healthspan messaging:** do not credit hours of manual work toward "zone 2 / fitness minutes" one-for-one. Show them as "dagelijkse belasting" and suggest recovery. The paradox literature supports keeping LTPA goals separate even for people who do heavy work.
- **For the 24-h HR mechanism:** the app can monitor the "elevated 24-h HR / low recovery" pattern directly (resting HR, HRV, sleep HR) after heavy work days. This turns the epidemiological mechanism into personal feedback.
- The paradox concerns sustained occupational exposure over years. A hobbyist doing occasional weekend carpentry is not the population studied, so the app should not show alarming messages for occasional DIY.

### Gaps
- No primary full text of the 2018 and 2021 papers was retrieved. The details come from abstracts and secondary summaries, and the 2021 citation details come from a mirror site.
- The search did not cover later critiques or meta-analyses (e.g. 2022–2025 papers questioning the paradox or showing that it attenuates after adjustment for socio-economic status). This is worth a follow-up search.
